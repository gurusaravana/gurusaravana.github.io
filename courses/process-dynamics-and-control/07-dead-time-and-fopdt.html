By the end of this lesson you can

  • Explain where dead time comes from
  • Fit a first-order plus dead-time model to step-test data
  • Use the Padé approximation

Notes

Dead time (time delay) $\theta$ is the time between a change in the input and the first sign of a change in the output. It comes from transport in pipes and conveyors, from analysers that take samples, and from many small lags in series.

$$G(s) = \frac{K e^{-\theta s}}{\tau s + 1}$$

This FOPDT model is the workhorse of industrial tuning because three numbers describe most self-regulating processes well enough to tune a PID controller.

Fitting from a step test (two-point method)

  1. Make a step of size $\Delta u$ with the controller in manual and record the output until it settles.
  2. Gain: $K = \Delta y_\infty / \Delta u$.
  3. Find the times $t_{28}$ and $t_{63}$ at which the output has made 28.3 % and 63.2 % of its final change (measured from the time of the step).
  4. $\tau = 1.5\,(t_{63} - t_{28})$ and $\theta = t_{63} - \tau$.

Why dead time is hard

During the dead time the controller sees no effect of what it has done, so it keeps pushing. The ratio $\theta/\tau$ (controllability ratio) tells us how hard a loop is: below 0.5 is easy, above 1 needs careful, slower tuning.

Padé approximation

For analysis we can replace the delay by a rational function: $e^{-\theta s} \approx \dfrac{1 - \theta s/2}{1 + \theta s/2}$.

Exercises

  1. Step test: $\Delta u = 10$ %, final $\Delta y = 25$ °C, $t_{28} = 7$ min, $t_{63} = 13$ min. Fit an FOPDT model.
    Show solution
    $K = 2.5$ °C/%. $\tau = 1.5(13 - 7) = 9$ min. $\theta = 13 - 9 = 4$ min.
  2. Liquid travels 30 m along a pipe at 1.5 m/s before reaching a sensor. What dead time does this add?
    Show solution
    $\theta = 30/1.5 = 20$ s.
  3. Which loop is harder to control: $\theta/\tau = 0.2$ or $\theta/\tau = 1.5$? Why?
    Show solution
    The second. The controller must wait longer to see the effect of its action relative to how fast the process responds, so it must act more cautiously.
