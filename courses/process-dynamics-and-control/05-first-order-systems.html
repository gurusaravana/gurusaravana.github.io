By the end of this lesson you can

  • Describe the step response of a first-order process
  • Read $K$ and $\tau$ from a step test
  • Recognise integrating (pure capacity) processes

Notes

The standard first-order model is

$$\tau\frac{dy}{dt} + y = Ku \qquad G(s) = \frac{K}{\tau s + 1}$$

Step response

For a step of size $M$ the output is $y(t) = KM\left(1 - e^{-t/\tau}\right)$.

  • The response never overshoots.
  • The initial slope is $KM/\tau$: if the process kept its initial speed it would finish in exactly one time constant.
  • The process "settles" (within 2 %) after about $4\tau$.

Integrating processes

A tank with a pump on the outlet (fixed outflow) has $A\,dh/dt = q_{in} - q_{out}$, so $G(s) = 1/(As)$. A step in inflow gives a ramp in level that never settles. These processes must be controlled.

Use the simulation: set $\theta = 0$, change $\tau$ and watch the gold dot. It always sits at 63.2 % of the final value, one time constant after the step.

Exercises

  1. A thermometer with $\tau = 6$ s is moved from 20 °C into boiling water at 100 °C. What does it read after 12 s?
    Show solution
    $T = 20 + 80(1 - e^{-2}) = 20 + 80(0.865) = 89.2$ °C.
  2. A step of +5 % in valve position raises a temperature from 60 °C to 72 °C, reaching 67.6 °C after 4 min. Find $K$ and $\tau$.
    Show solution
    $K = 12/5 = 2.4$ °C/%. 63.2 % of 12 is 7.6, i.e. 67.6 °C, reached at 4 min, so $\tau = 4$ min.
  3. Why does an integrating process have no steady-state gain?
    Show solution
    Because $G(0) = 1/(A\cdot 0)$ is infinite: any steady imbalance between inflow and outflow makes the level change forever.
