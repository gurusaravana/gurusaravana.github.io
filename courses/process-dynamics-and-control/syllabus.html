Process Dynamics and Control · EIE306

Syllabus

What the course covers, how it is assessed and what to read.

Course description

This course introduces the dynamic behaviour of chemical and industrial processes and the design of control systems that keep them safe, on specification and economical. Students build models from first principles, analyse them with Laplace transforms and frequency response, and design and tune feedback controllers. Interactive simulations are used throughout.

Prerequisites

Engineering mathematics (differential equations, complex numbers), and basic material and energy balances.

Learning outcomes

After completing the course, students will be able to:

  1. Develop dynamic models of processes from mass and energy balances
  2. Linearise models and express them as transfer functions
  3. Predict the response of first-order, second-order and dead-time processes
  4. Fit simple models to plant step-test data
  5. Design, tune and analyse PID feedback loops
  6. Choose feedforward, cascade and advanced control strategies for a given process

Textbooks

  • D. E. Seborg, T. F. Edgar, D. A. Mellichamp and F. J. Doyle III, Process Dynamics and Control, 4th ed., Wiley.
  • G. Stephanopoulos, Chemical Process Control: An Introduction to Theory and Practice, Prentice Hall.
  • D. R. Coughanowr and S. E. LeBlanc, Process Systems Analysis and Control, 3rd ed., McGraw-Hill.
  • B. A. Ogunnaike and W. H. Ray, Process Dynamics, Modeling, and Control, Oxford University Press.
