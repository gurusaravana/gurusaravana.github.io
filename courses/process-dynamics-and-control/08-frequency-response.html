By the end of this lesson you can

  • Find the amplitude ratio and phase lag of a process
  • Sketch and read Bode plots
  • Use gain and phase margins as stability measures

Notes

If a sine wave $u = A\sin\omega t$ enters a stable linear process, the output settles to a sine of the same frequency with a different amplitude and a phase shift. Substituting $s = j\omega$:

$$AR = |G(j\omega)|, \qquad \phi = \angle G(j\omega)$$

First-order process

$$AR = \frac{K}{\sqrt{1 + \omega^2\tau^2}}, \qquad \phi = -\tan^{-1}(\omega\tau)$$

Slow inputs ($\omega \ll 1/\tau$) pass with full gain. Fast inputs are filtered out. This is why a large tank smooths flow disturbances.

Bode plots

Plot $\log AR$ and $\phi$ against $\log\omega$. For series elements, amplitude ratios multiply (add on a log scale) and phases add. A dead time has $AR = 1$ but a phase of $-\omega\theta$ that grows without limit.

Stability margins

  • Crossover frequency $\omega_c$: where the open-loop phase is −180°.
  • Gain margin $GM = 1/AR_{OL}(\omega_c)$. Typical design: 1.7 to 2.
  • Phase margin: 180° plus the phase where $AR_{OL} = 1$. Typical design: 30° to 45°.

Exercises

  1. For $G = 2/(5s + 1)$ at $\omega = 0.2$ rad/min, find AR and $\phi$.
    Show solution
    $\omega\tau = 1$. $AR = 2/\sqrt2 = 1.41$, $\phi = -45°$.
  2. Why does dead time make feedback loops less stable at high gain?
    Show solution
    It adds phase lag $-\omega\theta$ without reducing the amplitude, so the −180° crossover happens at a lower frequency where the gain is still high.
  3. In the simulation, what happens to AR as $\omega$ increases with $\tau$ fixed?
    Show solution
    It decreases towards zero, and the phase lag approaches −90°.
