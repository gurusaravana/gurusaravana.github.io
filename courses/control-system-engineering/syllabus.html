Control System Engineering · EEE2510

Syllabus

From the 2026-27 course plan. L-T-P-C: 3-1-0-4 (3 lectures and 1 tutorial a week).

Course description

This course explores the importance of control system engineering and develops the basic abilities of modelling and analysing control systems. It is both conceptual and analytical, builds critical thinking and analytical skills, and strengthens programming and simulation skills through assignments.

📄 Download the full course plan (PDF)

Prerequisite

Signals and Systems.

Course outcomes

On successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

COStatementBloom's level
CO1Derive transfer function models of basic electrical and mechanical systems.Apply
CO2Solve for the transfer function of control systems using block diagram reduction and signal flow graphs by Mason's gain formula.Apply
CO3Analyse the time response of first and second-order systems for standard test inputs, and the effect of P, PI and PID controllers.Analyse
CO4Analyse the stability and performance of control systems using time-domain and frequency-domain approaches.Analyse
CO5Select lead, lag and lead-lag compensators to achieve desired performance specifications.Apply
CO6Determine state-space models and system properties for linear and nonlinear dynamic systems.Apply

Course content

ModuleTopicsSessions
1System components and their representation
Introduction to control systems, mathematical models of physical systems: differential equations of mechanical and electrical systems, block diagrams and signal flow graphs.		9L + 3T
2Time response analysis
Unit step response of first and second-order systems, time response specifications, steady-state errors and error constants, P, PI and PID controllers.		9L + 3T
3Stability analysis
Concept of stability, Routh stability criterion, root locus, frequency-domain specifications, Bode diagrams, stability from Bode plots, relative stability, introduction to the Nyquist criterion, lead, lag and lead-lag compensation.		15L + 5T
4State space analysis
Concept of state, state variables and state model, controllability and observability, introduction to nonlinear systems.		12L + 4T

How the course is taught

  • Lectures with slides, and problem-based learning in tutorials.
  • Simulation-based learning: MATLAB demonstrations for modelling (sessions 3–5), stability (27), frequency response (34) and compensation (42–45).
  • Flipped classroom for block diagram reduction (sessions 9–10).
  • Jigsaw peer learning for root locus and Bode plots (sessions 31–38).
