By the end of this lesson you can

  • Define frequency-domain specifications
  • Sketch asymptotic Bode magnitude and phase plots
  • Find gain and phase margins from a Bode plot

Notes

Put $s = j\omega$: a sine input gives a sine output scaled by $|G(j\omega)|$ and shifted by $\angle G(j\omega)$.

Frequency-domain specifications

  • Resonant peak $M_r = \dfrac{1}{2\zeta\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}$ at $\omega_r = \omega_n\sqrt{1 - 2\zeta^2}$ (for $\zeta < 0.707$).
  • Bandwidth: the frequency where the closed-loop gain falls to −3 dB.

Asymptotic Bode plot

FactorMagnitude slopePhase
Gain $K$Flat at $20\log K$0°
Pole at origin $1/s$−20 dB/decade−90°
Simple pole $1/(1 + sT)$0, then −20 dB/dec after $1/T$0 to −90°
Simple zero $(1 + sT)$0, then +20 dB/dec after $1/T$0 to +90°
Quadratic pole−40 dB/dec after $\omega_n$0 to −180°

Stability from the Bode plot

  • Gain crossover $\omega_{gc}$: where $|GH| = 0$ dB. Phase margin $= 180° + \angle GH(j\omega_{gc})$.
  • Phase crossover $\omega_{pc}$: where $\angle GH = -180°$. Gain margin $= -20\log|GH(j\omega_{pc})|$ dB.
  • For a minimum-phase system, both margins positive means stable. Typical targets: PM 30°–60°, GM above 6 dB.

Exercises

  1. Sketch the magnitude asymptotes of $G = 10/(s(1 + 0.1s))$.
    Show solution
    At $\omega = 1$: 20 dB, slope −20 dB/dec; corner at 10 rad/s, then −40 dB/dec. It crosses 0 dB near $\omega = 10$ rad/s.
  2. Find $M_r$ and $\omega_r$ for $\zeta = 0.4$, $\omega_n = 5$.
    Show solution
    $M_r = 1/(2\cdot0.4\cdot0.917) = 1.36$. $\omega_r = 5\sqrt{1 - 0.32} = 4.12$ rad/s.
  3. Why does a larger phase margin usually mean less overshoot?
    Show solution
    Phase margin is closely related to closed-loop damping: roughly $\zeta \approx PM/100$ for PM below about 70°.
