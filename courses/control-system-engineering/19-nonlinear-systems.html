By the end of this lesson you can

  • List common nonlinearities in control systems
  • Describe behaviours that only nonlinear systems show
  • Use the phase plane to visualise nonlinear motion

Notes

Real systems are only approximately linear. Superposition fails for nonlinear systems, so the methods of Modules 2 and 3 apply only near an operating point.

Common nonlinearities

NonlinearityWhere it appears
SaturationAmplifiers, valves at their limits, motor current limits
Dead zoneSmall inputs that produce no motion (static friction, motor start-up)
BacklashGear trains
Relay (on–off)Thermostats, bang-bang control
Coulomb frictionMechanical sliding contacts

Nonlinear behaviour

  • Several equilibrium points, each with its own stability.
  • Stability that depends on the size of the initial condition or input.
  • Limit cycles: sustained oscillations of fixed amplitude.
  • Jump resonance and harmonics in the output.

Phase plane

The phase plane plots velocity against position. Each initial condition gives a trajectory. The pendulum $\ddot\theta = -(g/L)\sin\theta - c\dot\theta$ is the classic example: small swings look linear, but large swings can go over the top.

In the simulation, drag the bob a little and let go, then try a large flick. Compare the trajectories in the phase portrait.

Exercises

  1. Linearise the pendulum about $\theta = 0$ and about $\theta = \pi$.
    Show solution
    Near 0: $\ddot\theta = -(g/L)\theta$, a stable oscillator. Near $\pi$: $\ddot\phi = +(g/L)\phi$ (with $\phi = \theta - \pi$), unstable.
  2. Give an example of saturation causing trouble in a PID loop.
    Show solution
    Integral windup: when the actuator saturates, the integral keeps growing and causes a large overshoot after the error changes sign.
  3. What is a limit cycle, and can a linear system have one?
    Show solution
    A sustained oscillation of fixed amplitude independent of initial conditions nearby. A linear system cannot: its oscillation amplitude always scales with the initial condition.

Practice problems from the textbook

From the end-of-chapter Problems in N. S. Nise, Control Systems Engineering, 7th ed. (Hoboken, NJ: John Wiley & Sons, 2015), Chapter 2 (Modeling in the Frequency Domain), Chapter 3 (Modeling in the Time Domain), Chapter 4 (Time Response). “Nise 4.24” means Chapter 4, Problem 24; § is the textbook section the problem practises. Work from the textbook statement and figure. The hints and short answers here are written for this course; full solutions are not reproduced.

  1. Nise 2.52 §2.10 Decide whether the straight-line relationship $c = 5r + 7$ is linear.
    Hint and answer
    Hint. Test both superposition and homogeneity. What is $c$ when $r = 0$?
    Answer. Not linear: doubling $r$ does not double $c$ because of the constant 7. It is a straight line but not a linear system.
  2. Nise 2.53 §2.10 Linearise a differential equation with input $f(x) = \sin x$ about $x = 0$ and about $x = \pi$.
    Hint and answer
    Hint. Use $f(x) \approx f(x_0) + f'(x_0)(x - x_0)$ with $\delta x = x - x_0$.
    Answer. Near $x = 0$, $\sin x \approx \delta x$. Near $x = \pi$, $\sin x \approx -\delta x$.
  3. Nise 2.54 §2.10 Write the differential equation for each range of a piecewise-linear $f(x)$ (Figure P2.34).
    Hint
    Hint. In each range $f(x)$ is a constant or a straight line, so the equation is linear inside that range.
  4. Nise 2.56 §2.10 Find $X(s)/F(s)$ for small excursions of a system with a nonlinear spring.
    Hint
    Hint. Find the operating point from the static force balance, then replace the spring by its slope $dF/dx$ at that point.
  5. Nise 3.19 §3.7 Represent a system with a nonlinear spring in state space, linearised about an operating point.
    Hint
    Hint. Write the nonlinear state equations first, then take the Jacobian at the equilibrium.
  6. Nise 4.37 §4.9 Use Simulink to see how amplifier saturation and gear backlash change a step response.
    Hint
    Hint. Compare the linear response with the saturated one: saturation slows the response because the input is clipped.
