By the end of this lesson you can

  • Describe lead, lag and lead-lag networks
  • Choose a compensator to meet a specification
  • Outline the Bode design procedure

Notes

$$G_c(s) = K_c\frac{s + z}{s + p}$$

Lead ($z < p$)Lag ($z > p$)
PhasePositive (adds phase)Negative (small lag)
Main effectMore phase margin, faster response, wider bandwidthHigher low-frequency gain, smaller steady-state error
Similar toPDPI
DrawbackAmplifies high-frequency noiseSlower response

Maximum lead phase $\phi_m = \sin^{-1}\dfrac{1 - \alpha}{1 + \alpha}$ (with $\alpha = z/p < 1$) at $\omega_m = \sqrt{zp}$.

Lead design on the Bode plot

  1. Choose $K_c$ to meet the steady-state error requirement.
  2. Find the existing phase margin.
  3. Required lead $\phi_m$ = desired PM − existing PM + 5° to 12° margin.
  4. Find $\alpha$, place $\omega_m$ at the new gain crossover (where the uncompensated gain is $-10\log(1/\alpha)$ dB).
  5. Check the result.

Lead-lag combines both: the lag part fixes steady-state error and the lead part fixes transient response.

Design in MATLAB with margin() and bode(), then compare the step responses before and after compensation.

Exercises

  1. A system needs 25° more phase margin. With a 5° safety margin, find $\alpha$ for a lead compensator.
    Show solution
    $\phi_m = 30°$: $\sin30° = 0.5 = (1-\alpha)/(1+\alpha) \Rightarrow \alpha = 1/3$.
  2. A system has a good transient response but too much steady-state error. Which compensator?
    Show solution
    Lag compensator: raises low-frequency gain while barely changing the phase near crossover.
  3. Why place the lag compensator's zero and pole well below the gain crossover frequency?
    Show solution
    So its phase lag does not reduce the phase margin; near crossover it then acts almost like a unity gain.

Practice problems from the textbook

From the end-of-chapter Problems in N. S. Nise, Control Systems Engineering, 7th ed. (Hoboken, NJ: John Wiley & Sons, 2015), Chapter 9 (Design via Root Locus), Chapter 11 (Design via Frequency Response). “Nise 4.24” means Chapter 4, Problem 24; § is the textbook section the problem practises. Work from the textbook statement and figure. The hints and short answers here are written for this course; full solutions are not reproduced.

  1. Nise 9.13 §9.3 Design a compensator to shorten the settling time of a system that runs at 20% overshoot.
    Hint
    Hint. Ideal derivative (PD) or lead compensation. Choose the new dominant pole, then use the angle condition to place the zero.
  2. Nise 9.19 §9.4 Design a lag-lead compensator for a fourth-order plant.
    Hint
    Hint. Design the lead part first for the transient, then the lag part for the steady-state error.
  3. Nise 9.21 §9.4 Design a lag-lead compensator for an uncompensated type 1 system.
    Hint
    Hint. Keep the lag pole and zero close together and close to the origin so the dominant poles hardly move.
  4. Nise 11.1 §11.2 Find the gain $K$ that gives a 10 dB gain margin.
    Hint
    Hint. Find the phase crossover frequency first; it does not depend on $K$.
  5. Nise 11.3 §11.2 Use frequency response to find $K$ for 20% overshoot.
    Hint
    Hint. Convert 20% overshoot to $\zeta$, then to the required phase margin, and adjust $K$ until the Bode plot has that margin.
  6. Nise 11.8 §11.3 Design a lag compensator for a 45° phase margin and a given static error constant.
    Hint
    Hint. Set $K$ for the error constant, find the frequency with the required phase, and put the lag zero one decade below it.
  7. Nise 11.12 §11.4 Design a compensator for a given $K_v$ and phase margin.
    Hint
    Hint. Lead compensation: maximum phase lead $\phi_{max}$ from $\sin\phi_{max} = (1-\beta)/(1+\beta)$, placed at the new gain crossover.
