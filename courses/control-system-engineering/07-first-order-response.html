Notes

Module 2 is about time response: what the output of a control system does, moment by moment, after the input changes. Every control system has to solve two problems over time. It must get there safely (the transient response: speed and stability, like a lift moving between floors without a jolt) and then stay there accurately (the steady-state response: tracking accuracy, like an autopilot holding a set altitude).

Transient and steady-state response

The total response is the sum of two parts:

$$c(t) = c_{tr}(t) + c_{ss}(t)$$

  • Transient response $c_{tr}(t)$: the initial, dynamic reaction as the system tries to reach the desired output. It varies strongly with time and dies away in a stable system.
  • Steady-state response $c_{ss}(t)$: the long-term behaviour after the transient oscillations have completely decayed.

Poles, zeros and the characteristic equation

Write a transfer function as $G(s) = N(s)/D(s)$.

  • Zeros are the roots of the numerator $N(s)$.
  • Poles are the roots of the denominator $D(s)$. Setting the closed-loop denominator to zero gives the characteristic equation; its roots are the closed-loop poles.
  • The number of closed-loop poles (the highest power of $s$ in the denominator) is the order of the system.

The roots of the characteristic equation are the poles of the system. They act as the "DNA" of its time response: from their positions alone you can predict speed, overshoot and stability.

The first-order system

The simplest dynamic model is $\dfrac{C(s)}{R(s)} = \dfrac{a}{s + a}$, with one pole at $s = -a$. The position of that pole completely governs the natural response. For a unit step, $C(s) = \dfrac{a}{s(s + a)}$ and

$$c(t) = 1 - e^{-at}$$

MetricFormulaMeaning
Time constant $\tau$$\tau = 1/a$Time to reach 63.2 % of the final value; the initial slope is $a = 1/\tau$.
Rise time $T_r$$T_r = \dfrac{2.2}{a} = 2.2\tau$Time to go from 10 % to 90 % of the final value.
Settling time $T_s$$T_s = \dfrac{4}{a} = 4\tau$Time to reach and stay within 2 % of the final value ($3\tau$ for 5 %).

For a unit ramp the output is $c(t) = t - \tau + \tau e^{-t/\tau}$, which lags the input by a steady error of $\tau$.

Set the dead time to zero in the simulation. Change $\tau$ and check that the gold dot always sits at 63.2 % of the final value.

Exercises

  1. A first-order system reaches 95 % of its final value in 6 s. Find $T$.
    Show solution
    $1 - e^{-6/T} = 0.95 \Rightarrow 6/T = 3 \Rightarrow T = 2$ s.
  2. Find the steady-state error of $1/(Ts + 1)$ to a unit ramp.
    Show solution
    $e_{ss} = T$, from $c(t) = t - T + Te^{-t/T}$.
  3. A thermometer with $T = 10$ s is put into a bath whose temperature rises at 1 °C/s. What is its lag after a long time?
    Show solution
    $e_{ss} = T \times 1 = 10$ °C below the bath temperature.
  4. A first-order system is $G(s) = \dfrac{5}{s + 5}$. Find the time constant, rise time and settling time (2 %).
    Show solution
    $a = 5$, so $\tau = 0.2$ s, $T_r = 2.2/5 = 0.44$ s and $T_s = 4/5 = 0.8$ s.
  5. Find the type of each open-loop system: (a) $\dfrac{s + 4}{(s + 6)(s^2 + 4s + 8)}$, (b) $\dfrac{15}{s(s^2 + 3s + 12)}$, (c) $\dfrac{s + 5}{s^2(s + 3)(s + 7)}$.
    Show solution
    Count the poles at $s = 0$: (a) none, type 0; (b) one, type 1; (c) two, type 2.
  6. A closed-loop transfer function is $\dfrac{10}{s^3 + 6s^2 + 11s + 6}$. What is its order, and where are its poles?
    Show solution
    Third order. $s^3 + 6s^2 + 11s + 6 = (s + 1)(s + 2)(s + 3)$, so the poles are at $-1$, $-2$ and $-3$.
