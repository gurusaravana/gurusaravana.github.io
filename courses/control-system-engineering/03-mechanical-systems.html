Notes

Translational elements

ElementForceLaplace form
Mass $M$$M\,\ddot x$$Ms^2X(s)$
Damper $B$$B\,\dot x$$BsX(s)$
Spring $K$$Kx$$KX(s)$

Mass–spring–damper

A force $f(t)$ acts on a mass held by a spring and a damper. Summing forces on the mass:

$$M\ddot x + B\dot x + Kx = f(t) \quad\Rightarrow\quad \frac{X(s)}{F(s)} = \frac{1}{Ms^2 + Bs + K}$$

Rotational elements

Inertia $J\ddot\theta$, rotational damping $B\dot\theta$ and torsional spring $K\theta$ play the same roles, with torque $T$ in place of force.

Analogies

So the mass–spring–damper behaves exactly like the series RLC circuit of Lesson 2.

Exercises

  1. Two masses: $M_1$ connected to a wall by spring $K_1$, and to $M_2$ by spring $K_2$. Force $f$ acts on $M_2$. Write the equations.
    Show solution
    $M_1\ddot x_1 + K_1x_1 + K_2(x_1 - x_2) = 0$ and $M_2\ddot x_2 + K_2(x_2 - x_1) = f$.
  2. For $M = 1$ kg and $K = 10$ N/m, what damping gives critical damping?
    Show solution
    $B = 2\sqrt{MK} = 2\sqrt{10} = 6.32$ N·s/m.
  3. Write the force–voltage analogous circuit of $M\ddot x + B\dot x + Kx = f$.
    Show solution
    Series RLC circuit with $L = M$, $R = B$, $C = 1/K$, driven by voltage $v = f$; the loop charge $q$ corresponds to $x$.
