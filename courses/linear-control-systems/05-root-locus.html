Notes

The transient response of a closed loop is set by the location of its closed-loop poles. When a loop gain $K$ can be adjusted, the poles move. The root locus is the path of the closed-loop poles as $K$ goes from 0 to $\infty$. It shows at a glance whether the loop is stable, how damped it is, and whether a gain change is enough or a compensator (extra poles and zeros) is needed.

A first example

For $G(s) = \dfrac{K}{s(s + 2)}$ with unity feedback, the characteristic equation is $s^2 + 2s + K = 0$, with roots $s = -1 \pm \sqrt{1 - K}$.

  • $K = 0$: the poles are the open-loop poles 0 and $-2$.
  • $0 < K < 1$: two real poles moving toward each other (overdamped).
  • $K = 1$: both at $-1$ (critically damped). This is a break-away point.
  • $K > 1$: $-1 \pm j\sqrt{K - 1}$. The real part stays at $-1$ (same settling time) while the imaginary part grows (more overshoot). The loop is never unstable.

Observations: there are as many branches as poles; branches start at the open-loop poles and end at the open-loop zeros (some at infinity).

Angle and magnitude conditions

The closed-loop poles satisfy $1 + KG(s)H(s) = 0$, that is $KG(s)H(s) = -1$. Split into two conditions:

$$\angle G(s)H(s) = \sum\angle(s + z_i) - \sum\angle(s + p_j) = (2k + 1)180°, \qquad K = \frac{\prod|s + p_j|}{\prod|s + z_i|}$$

A point $s_1$ is on the locus if the angle condition holds there; the magnitude condition then gives the gain at that point. Geometrically, $K$ = (product of lengths from the poles to $s_1$) / (product of lengths from the zeros to $s_1$).

Construction rules

  1. Start and end. The $n$ branches start ($K = 0$) at the open-loop poles and end ($K \to \infty$) at the $m$ open-loop zeros; $n - m$ branches go to infinity.
  2. Symmetry. The locus is symmetrical about the real axis.
  3. Real axis. A point on the real axis is on the locus if the number of real poles plus zeros to its right is odd.
  4. Asymptotes. The $n - m$ far branches follow straight lines at angles $\theta_a = \dfrac{(2k + 1)180°}{n - m}$ from the centroid $\sigma_a = \dfrac{\sum \text{poles} - \sum \text{zeros}}{n - m}$.
  5. Break-away and break-in points are double roots: solve $\dfrac{dK}{ds} = 0$, or equivalently $N(s)D'(s) - N'(s)D(s) = 0$ for $GH = N/D$. Keep only roots that lie on the locus. $k$ branches meeting at a point separate at $180°/k$.
  6. Angle of departure from a complex pole: $\theta_d = 180° + \sum\angle(\text{from zeros}) - \sum\angle(\text{from other poles})$. Angle of arrival at a complex zero: $\theta_a = 180° - \sum\angle(\text{from other zeros}) + \sum\angle(\text{from poles})$.
  7. Imaginary-axis crossing. Use Routh: the gain that makes a row zero is the critical gain, and the auxiliary equation gives the crossing frequency.
  8. Gain at a point: use the magnitude condition.

Example 1: departure angle and jω crossing

  • Poles $0$, $-1 \pm j2$; zero $-4$. Real-axis locus between 0 and $-4$.
  • Asymptotes: $n - m = 2$, angles $\pm 90°$, centroid $\sigma_a = \dfrac{(0 - 1 - 1) - (-4)}{2} = +1$. The two complex branches head into the right half-plane.
  • Departure from $-1 + j2$: from the zero, $\tan^{-1}(2/3) = 33.7°$; from the pole at 0, $180° - \tan^{-1}2 = 116.6°$; from $-1 - j2$, $90°$. So $\theta_d = 180° + 33.7° - 116.6° - 90° = 7.1°$.
  • Crossing: $s^3 + 2s^2 + (5 + K)s + 4K = 0$. The $s^1$ entry is $5 - K$, so $K = 5$; then $2s^2 + 20 = 0$ gives $s = \pm j3.16$.

So the loop is stable for $0 < K < 5$.

Example 2: break-away point

  • Poles 0, $-3$, $-3$. The whole negative real axis is on the locus (one pole to the right of $(-3, 0)$; three to the left of $-3$).
  • Asymptotes at $60°$, $180°$, $300°$ from $\sigma_a = (-3 - 3 - 0)/3 = -2$.
  • $K = -(s^3 + 6s^2 + 9s)$, so $\dfrac{dK}{ds} = -(3s^2 + 12s + 9) = 0$, giving $(s + 1)(s + 3) = 0$. The break-away point is $s = -1$ with $K = 4$ ($s = -3$ is the double pole itself, $K = 0$).
  • Crossing: $s^3 + 6s^2 + 9s + K$; $6 \times 9 = K$ gives $K = 54$ at $\pm j3$.

Example 3: a fourth-order locus

  • Poles $0$, $-4$, $-2 \pm j4$. Real-axis segment from 0 to $-4$.
  • Asymptotes at $\pm45°$ and $\pm135°$ from $\sigma_a = (0 - 4 - 2 - 2)/4 = -2$.
  • Break points: $\dfrac{d}{ds}(s^4 + 8s^3 + 36s^2 + 80s) = 4(s^3 + 6s^2 + 18s + 20) = 0$, so $(s + 2)(s^2 + 4s + 10) = 0$: $s = -2$ ($K = 64$) and $s = -2 \pm j2.45$ ($K = 100$). All three are on the locus; at each, branches meet and leave at $90°$.
  • Crossing: $s^4 + 8s^3 + 36s^2 + 80s + K$. Routh: $s^2$ row $26, K$; $s^1$ entry $80 - 8K/26$, zero at $K = 260$. Then $26s^2 + 260 = 0$ gives $\pm j3.16$.

At $K = 100$ the characteristic polynomial is exactly $(s^2 + 4s + 10)^2$: two pairs of equal roots, which is why the branches from the real axis and from the complex poles collide there.

Root contours: varying another parameter

The root locus works for any parameter that enters the characteristic equation linearly. For $G(s) = \dfrac{10(1 + \tau s)}{s(s + 2)}$, the characteristic equation $s^2 + 2s + 10 + 10\tau s = 0$ can be rewritten as

$$1 + \tau\,\frac{10s}{s^2 + 2s + 10} = 0$$

which is a root locus in $\tau$ with "poles" $-1 \pm j3$ and a "zero" at the origin.

  • Departure from $-1 + j3$: $\theta_d = 180° + \angle(\text{from zero}) - \angle(\text{from other pole}) = 180° + 108.4° - 90° = 198.4°$, which is the same direction as $-161.6°$.
  • Break-in: $\dfrac{d}{ds}\left(\dfrac{s^2 + 2s + 10}{s}\right) = 0$ gives $s^2 = 10$, $s = -3.16$, reached when $\tau = 0.43$.

The original slide quotes $\theta_d = -198°$. The sign is slipped: $-198°$ would point into the right half-plane. The correct value is $+198.4°$ (that is, $-161.6°$), which points down and to the left, as the plot shows.

Exercises

  1. Sketch the root locus of $\dfrac{K(s + 2)}{s^2 + 2s + 3}$. Find the departure angle and the break-in point.
    Show solution
    Poles $-1 \pm j\sqrt{2}$, zero $-2$. $\theta_d = 180° + \tan^{-1}(\sqrt{2}/1) - 90° = 180° + 54.7° - 90° = 144.7°$. Break-in: $s^2 + 4s + 1 = 0$, $s = -2 - \sqrt{3} = -3.73$ ($K = 5.46$). The locus is a circle of radius $\sqrt{3}$ about the zero; one branch then goes to $-2$, the other to $-\infty$.
  2. For $\dfrac{K(s + 3)}{s(s + 1)(s + 2)(s + 4)}$, find the asymptotes and the break-away point.
    Show solution
    $n - m = 3$: angles $60°$, $180°$, $300°$; $\sigma_a = \dfrac{(0 - 1 - 2 - 4) - (-3)}{3} = -4/3$. Break-away between 0 and $-1$ at $s = -0.435$ ($K = 0.535$).
  3. For $\dfrac{K(s + 1)}{s(s + 2)(s + 4)^2}$, find the centroid and the break-away point.
    Show solution
    $\sigma_a = \dfrac{(-2 - 8) - (-1)}{3} = -3$. The real-axis segments are $(-1, 0)$, $(-4, -2)$ and $(-\infty, -4)$ (the double pole at $-4$ counts twice). The break-away point is $s = -2.60$, between the poles at $-2$ and $-4$ ($K = 1.91$).
  4. For $\dfrac{K(s + 3)}{(s + 1)(s + 2)(s + 4)}$, find the break-away point.
    Show solution
    Real-axis segments $(-2, -1)$ and $(-4, -3)$. Break-away at $s = -1.53$ ($K = 0.42$); the branches then follow $\pm90°$ asymptotes from $\sigma_a = (-7 + 3)/2 = -2$. (The slide's $-1.55$ is a graphical estimate.)
  5. For $\dfrac{K(s - 3)(s - 5)}{(s + 1)(s + 2)}$, find the break-away and break-in points, and the range of $K$ for stability.
    Show solution
    Break-away at $-1.45$ (between the poles), break-in at $+3.82$ (between the zeros). The characteristic equation is $(1 + K)s^2 + (3 - 8K)s + (2 + 15K) = 0$, stable while $3 - 8K > 0$, i.e. $0 < K < 0.375$; at $K = 0.375$ the locus crosses the $j\omega$ axis at $\pm j2.36$.
  6. For $\dfrac{K(s + 1)}{s^2(s + 3.6)}$, find the centroid of the asymptotes. Is $-1.65 + j0.937$ on the locus?
    Show solution
    $\sigma_a = \dfrac{(0 + 0 - 3.6) - (-1)}{2} = -1.3$. Angle at $-1.65 + j0.937$: from the zero $180° - 55.3° = 124.7°$; from the double pole $2 \times (180° - 29.6°) = 300.8°$; from $-3.6$: $25.7°$. Net $124.7° - 300.8° - 25.7° = -201.8°$, not an odd multiple of $180°$, so the point is not on the locus.
